Hello! I'm excited to help you understand Fourier's Law and heat conduction. Let's start with the **core idea**:

**What is Fourier's Law?**
Fourier's Law tells us how heat flows through a material. Think of it like this: if one side of a metal rod is hot and the other side is cold, heat naturally travels from hot to cold. Fourier's Law describes *how fast* that happens.

**The Key Formula:**
Q = -k × A × (dT/dx)

Where:
- **Q** = heat flow rate (how much heat moves per second)
- **k** = thermal conductivity (how good the material is at conducting heat)
- **A** = cross-sectional area (the size of the surface heat passes through)
- **dT/dx** = temperature gradient (how quickly temperature changes across distance)

**Physical Intuition:**
Imagine holding a metal spoon in hot soup. Heat travels up the handle toward your hand. The steeper the temperature difference between the soup and your hand, the faster heat flows. A better conductor (like metal) lets heat flow faster than a poor conductor (like wood).
